# Millennium Typer: the typing test's passages
#
# The test picks one of these at random. Each is a paragraph: lines next to each other join into one passage, and
# a blank line starts the next. Anything after a # is a note and isn't part of the text.
# Aim for about 250 characters (the game refuses anything under 150, or over 300, which no longer fits the
# screen), using only what every keyboard can type: plain letters, digits, spaces and punctuation. Capitals don't
# need Shift: the test ignores case.
#
# Drafts for Jeremy to pick from or edit. Delete the ones you don't want.

# 1. The original's story, retold
An evil emperor from a distant star has sent his fleet to destroy Earth. Our people built one last ship to stop
him: the Millennium, a fighter that obeys its pilot's words. Every word you type sends a missile at an enemy.
Type fast, type true, and Earth may live.

# 2. The warning
The warning came from the edge of the solar system: a fleet of a thousand ships, led by an emperor who has never
lost a war. Earth has one answer. Its pilots do not steer or aim. They type, and the Millennium turns every word
they write into fire.

# 3. The briefing (doubles as a tutorial)
Pilot, listen closely. Every enemy ship carries a code word. Type it and your missiles find their mark. A
shielded ship needs a short word first to knock out its shield. If your hull is hit, type the repair word. Keep
calm and keep your hands on the keys.

# 4. The last ship
Year one thousand of the new calendar. The emperor's armada has crossed the void, and the old defences have
already fallen. Only the Millennium remains, a ship built to move as fast as human thought. Its weapons wait for
one thing: your words.

# 5. The doubters
They laughed when the engineers said a warship could be flown from a keyboard. They stopped laughing when a pilot
typing ninety words a minute shot down the first enemy scout. Now the emperor's fleet is coming, and Earth needs
every fast pair of hands it has.
